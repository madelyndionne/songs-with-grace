Devotional

Before We Begin

The song, Be Still And Know, draws its central images from Psalm 46: a world in upheaval, waters in motion, nations in conflict, and God remaining utterly unchanged. The song reflects those images, but Psalm 46 is the authority. It is the Scripture that tells us what our confidence rests upon.

When Everything Moves

Psalm 46 does not describe peace by pretending that nothing frightening is happening. The psalm imagines the earth changing, mountains moving, waters roaring, kingdoms shaking, and war reaching across the land (Psalm 46:1–3, 6, 9).

Against that background comes the opening truth: God is our refuge and strength, a present help in trouble (Psalm 46:1).

That distinction matters. The promise is not that trouble will never come. The promise is that trouble does not remove God from His place, nor His people from His care.

Fear often tells us that stability depends upon circumstances becoming stable again. Psalm 46 points somewhere deeper. Even if the landscape itself changes, God has not.

A River in the Middle of the Noise

Then the psalm changes its imagery. After roaring waters and moving mountains, Psalm 46:4–5 speaks of a river whose streams make glad the city of God and of God being present in the midst of it.

The contrast is striking. Outside, creation and nations are in turmoil. In the midst of God's presence, there is another source of life.

Scripture repeatedly uses water as an image of God's sustaining provision and life (Psalm 1:3; Isaiah 58:11; John 4:14). Psalm 46 does not require the surrounding world to become quiet before God's presence can sustain His people.

That is a useful truth when our own lives feel noisy. Peace with God is not necessarily the absence of motion around us. It is the security of knowing Who is present within it.

What “Be Still” Asks of Us

Psalm 46:10 brings the great command at the center of the song: be still and know that He is God.

In context, this is not merely an invitation to find a quiet room or calm our breathing, though those things may help us listen. The psalm has just described God's power over conflict and His ability to bring wars to an end (Psalm 46:8–9). The call to be still is a call to stop striving as though the final outcome rests in human hands and to recognize God's rule.

We are not being asked to deny the storm.

We are being asked to remember who is God in the storm.

There are times when faithful action is required of us. There are also times when our frantic effort begins to reveal that we have quietly assumed responsibility for things that belong to God alone. Psalm 46 calls us back to the proper order.

The Lord Is With Us

Psalm 46 repeats the same assurance in verses 7 and 11: the Lord of hosts is with His people; the God of Jacob is their refuge.

The repetition is worth noticing. The psalm does not end by explaining every disaster. It returns to God's presence.

That same truth runs throughout Scripture. God promised His presence to His people in times of fear (Isaiah 41:10). Jesus promised His disciples that He would be with them as they carried out His commission (Matthew 28:20). Hebrews 13:5–6 directs believers to confidence rooted in God's promise not to forsake them.

When the song reaches its closing words—“Through all of this I am with you”—it points toward that larger biblical testimony. Our security is not that we can hold everything together. It is that God remains faithful.

Reflection

What in my life currently feels as though it is moving beneath my feet?

Am I asking God only to make the circumstances quiet, or am I also learning to recognize His presence while they are still unsettled?

Is there something I am trying to control that I need to place again into God's hands?

What would it mean today for me to be still and remember that He is God?

Prayer

Father, when the world around me feels uncertain, remind me that You have not changed. Be my refuge when I am afraid and my strength when I am tired. Help me distinguish between what You have given me to do and what I am trying to carry as though it belonged to me. Teach my heart to be still before You, to trust Your presence, and to remember that You remain God over every circumstance. Amen.
