Verse 1

The water whispers the secrets that start to shine—

Wisdom carved in stone leaves echoes behind.

The call of the current—rough, ancient, and wild—

Teaches me: that to flow at all— is to listen like a child.

Chorus 1

For my own tributary I pay Him tribute now.

For every tear of yearning, every why and how—

He gave me courage and put a future before me—

In my tributary, His Spirit flows through me.

Verse 2

I met the streams where songs of the greats reside.

They hesitate to welcome me and my whitewater tide.

I learned about harmony, how to refine my sound,

He gently reminds me, “Stay true to Who you found.”

Chorus 2

For my own tributary I pay Him tribute now.

For every tear of yearning, every why and how—

He gave me courage and put a future before me—

In my tributary, His Spirit flows through me.

Verse 3

If I never made a sound or dared to write a word,

My stream’s bed would dry, nothing would be heard.

No trickling water left, no eddies to unwind—

Currents need a Spirit. His Spirit's in my mind.

Bridge

Every current knows its source, yet forgets where it will end;

The way is shaped by every twist, by every turn and bend.

I was never meant to guide this flow, or keep its force confined—

Just to let its flow move through me, refining heart and mind.

Final Chorus

I love my tributary and I honor where it’s from,

Each song, lyric, and devotion is my way of coming home.

I’ll let it join the ocean’s song, but keep the Spirit of Thee—

That keeps my tributary flowing, unbridled, wild, and free.

Outro

He fills my tributary—

He is the One who sets it free,

He is the One who flows through me.

Devotional

Know the Source, Let It Flow

A tributary is not its own source. It carries what it has received.

That simple fact gives us a useful way to think about the gifts, callings, opportunities, and words God places in our lives. We may cultivate them. We may become more skillful in using them. We may learn from others and refine what we do. But Scripture keeps bringing us back to the Source.

James 1:17 reminds us that every good gift comes from above. First Corinthians 12:4–7 says that there are different gifts and different kinds of service, yet the same Spirit is at work through them for the good of others. What we carry matters. But it is not self-created.

Living Water Comes From God

Jesus repeatedly used water to speak about the life God gives. In John 4:13–14, He describes the water He gives as becoming a spring within a person. In John 7:37–39, John explains Jesus’ words about living water as a reference to the Holy Spirit.

Scripture therefore gives us more than a beautiful water metaphor. It tells us where spiritual life originates. We do not manufacture the Spirit’s presence through effort, talent, or emotion. We receive from God.

That changes the question from “How do I create something powerful?” to “Am I remaining close to the One from whom life flows?”

Refinement Is Not Replacement

Growth in skill is good. Scripture does not praise carelessness. Romans 12:6 calls believers to use the gifts given to them according to grace, and throughout Scripture we see people learning, practicing, preparing, and becoming more capable in the work entrusted to them.

But refinement can become dangerous if we begin mistaking another person’s stream for our own.

We can learn from people whose work is deeper, stronger, more polished, or more established than ours. Their example may teach us discipline, harmony, structure, patience, or excellence. But God has not called us to become copies of them.

The goal is not to sound like the greatest stream we have encountered. The goal is faithfulness to the Source who gave us our own place to flow.

What Flows Is Meant to Move

Water that is continually received but never released does not behave like a tributary. It pools. It stagnates. A tributary remains a tributary because it moves.

Isaiah 55:10–11 describes rain and snow coming down from heaven, accomplishing their purpose, and compares that movement with God’s Word going forth to accomplish what He intends. Ezekiel 47:1–12 gives another striking picture: water flows outward from the temple, becoming deeper as it goes, and bringing life wherever the river reaches.

These passages do not promise that every idea we have comes from God or that every creative impulse carries divine authority. Scripture remains the measure. But they do show a God whose gifts and truth are not sterile. What comes from Him bears fruit according to His purpose.

Sometimes stewardship therefore means release. We receive, discern, prepare, and then allow what God has entrusted to us to move beyond us.

The Current Is Not Ours to Control

There is a difference between stewardship and control.

We are responsible for obedience, discernment, preparation, honesty, and care. But we cannot force the outcome. First Corinthians 3:6–7 makes that distinction plainly: one person plants, another waters, but God gives the increase.

That can be freeing. We do not need to know where every current will end before we allow it to move. We do not need to guarantee how another person will receive what we offer. We do not need to make ourselves responsible for results that belong to God.

Faithfulness asks us to carry the water well. God determines where it finally goes.

Joining Something Greater

A tributary does not lose its history when it reaches a larger river. Every bend, stone, narrow passage, and stretch of whitewater has helped shape what arrives there. Yet the tributary was never meant to remain isolated forever.

The same can be true of our gifts. What God gives one person is never the whole of His work. First Corinthians 12 describes one body with many members. No one gift, voice, ministry, song, or devotional is the entire river.

Our part can remain recognizable while joining something much greater than ourselves.

That is where humility and freedom meet: we can value the particular stream God has entrusted to us without mistaking it for the ocean.

Reflection

What has God entrusted to me that I may have begun treating as though it originated with me?

Am I learning from others in a way that refines my gift—or in a way that tempts me to abandon the voice and calling God has given me?

Is there something I have been holding too tightly because I want to control where it goes or what it accomplishes?

What would faithful release look like today?

Prayer

Father, every good thing I have comes from You. Keep me close to the Source. Teach me to receive with gratitude, to refine what You have entrusted to me with humility, and to release it without trying to control what belongs to You. By Your Spirit, keep what You have placed in me alive and moving. Let my small stream serve Your larger purpose, and keep me faithful wherever You lead it. Amen.
