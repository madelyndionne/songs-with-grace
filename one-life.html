Devotional

Before We Begin

The song, One Life, grows from Galatians 2:20 and the startling truth Paul describes there: the believer's life is bound to Christ so completely that the old self is spoken of as crucified with Him, while the life that remains is lived by faith in the Son of God. The song reflects that truth; Scripture defines it.

A Righteousness We Could Not Produce

There is a reason the first verse begins with inability. Scripture does not present salvation as the reward for finally becoming righteous enough. Romans 3:20–24 closes that door plainly: human works cannot establish our righteousness before God, while justification is given by grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.

Philippians 3:8–9 makes the contrast personal. Paul refuses to place his confidence in a righteousness of his own and instead rests in the righteousness that comes through faith in Christ.

That does not make righteousness unimportant. It tells us where our right standing with God comes from.

We do not manufacture it and then bring it to Christ. We come to Christ because we do not have it.

Crucified With Christ

Galatians 2:20 does not describe physical death. Paul is speaking about a decisive change in identity and allegiance. The self that sought life through its own standing before the law no longer has the final word. Christ has become the center of the believer's life.

Romans 6:3–11 develops the same pattern through union with Christ in His death and resurrection. The believer is called to reckon the old life dead to sin and to live unto God through Jesus Christ.

That is why the chorus can hold two truths together: “I am crucified with Christ,” and “yet—I live.” Christianity is not the erasure of the person. It is life reordered around Christ.

The Life I Now Live

Galatians 2:20 immediately answers the question raised by the second verse: What do I do with the life He saved? Paul says that the life he now lives in the flesh, he lives by faith in the Son of God.

Faith is therefore not only the doorway into Christian life. It is the continuing posture of that life.

We trust Christ for what we could never accomplish ourselves, and we continue trusting Him as we learn obedience, surrender, and dependence. Romans 12:1–2 calls believers to present themselves to God and to be transformed. Ephesians 2:10 reminds us that the people saved by grace are also created in Christ Jesus unto good works.

Grace does not leave us unchanged. It changes the reason we obey.

Made Righteous—and Still Being Changed

One line in the song, One Life, says, “I can stop trying to be righteous. He has already made me so.” Scripture helps us hear that carefully.

In justification, the believer's right standing before God rests in Christ rather than in personal merit (Romans 5:1; 2 Corinthians 5:21). We can stop trying to earn the verdict that Christ has secured.

But Scripture also calls those who belong to Christ to pursue holiness and to grow in obedience (Philippians 2:12–13; Hebrews 12:14). We do not pursue holiness in order to purchase salvation. We pursue it because God is already at work in those He has saved.

The difference is enormous. One life is driven by the fear that we must make ourselves acceptable. The other is lived from the security of what Christ has already done.

Not My Own

The bridge reaches another New Testament theme: the saved life belongs to the One who saved it. First Corinthians 6:19–20 tells believers that they are not their own because they were bought with a price. Second Corinthians 5:14–15 says that Christ died so that those who live should no longer live unto themselves, but unto Him.

Surrender, then, is not payment for salvation. It is the response of a life that has been redeemed.

The question is no longer, “How can I save my life?” Christ has answered that.

The question becomes, “How shall I live the life He has given me?”

Reflection

Where am I still trying to establish my standing with God through my own performance?

What would it look like today to live by faith rather than by self-reliance?

Is there an area of my life I still treat as entirely my own, rather than something entrusted to Christ?

How does knowing that obedience grows from grace change the way I approach holiness?

Prayer

Father, thank You for the life of Christ given for me and for the righteousness I could never produce on my own. Teach me what it means to be crucified with Christ and yet truly alive in Him. Keep me from trying to earn what You have given by grace, and keep me from using grace as an excuse to remain unchanged. Let the life I now live be lived by faith, surrendered to You, and shaped by Christ who lives in me. Amen.

United with Christ in death and new life: Romans 6:3–11

Saved by grace and created for good works: Ephesians 2:8–10

Surrender and transformation: Romans 12:1–2; Philippians 2:12–13

Bought with a price; living for Christ: 1 Corinthians 6:19–20; 2 Corinthians 5:14–15

Called to holiness: Hebrews 12:14
