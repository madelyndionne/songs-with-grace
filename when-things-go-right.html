Devotional

When Things Go Right — The Joy of a Calling Fulfilled

We often turn to God most urgently when something goes wrong. We ask for strength in trouble, wisdom in uncertainty, and comfort in sorrow. But what happens when things go right? When we recognize that God has given us meaningful work, when we see someone helped, and when the work itself becomes a source of joy? Scripture has something to say about that season, too.

The song, When Things Go Right, begins with a question that is easy to overlook: can we recognize God’s grace in a peaceful, fruitful season as readily as we recognize our need for Him in a difficult one? James 1:17 reminds us that “Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above.” Gratitude does not require us to pretend life has no difficulties. It invites us to notice the Giver when good things are happening.

Jesus told His disciples, “Herein is my Father glorified, that ye bear much fruit” (John 15:8, KJV). Yet He had already made clear where fruitfulness begins: “for without me ye can do nothing” (John 15:5, KJV). The fruit is not proof that we have become sufficient in ourselves. It is a reason to remain close to Christ. Even when our hands do the work, the life and strength behind lasting spiritual fruit come from Him.

This is why the refrain is the heart of the song: “The joy isn’t just what You called us to, / It’s knowing we are doing it for You.” Colossians 3:23–24 tells believers to work “heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men” and reminds them, “for ye serve the Lord Christ.” The same act can feel entirely different when it is offered to God rather than performed merely for recognition. A quiet visit, a kind word, a song, a meal, or a patient hour of service may be unseen by most people. It is not unseen by Him.

The third verse speaks of seeing fruit and feeling the exquisite joy of it. Jesus connected obedience and abiding love with joy: “that my joy might remain in you, and that your joy might be full” (John 15:11, KJV). Paul, too, described the believers he served as his “joy and crown” (Philippians 4:1, KJV). It is not wrong to rejoice when another person is encouraged or strengthened. The important question is where that joy leads us: toward self-congratulation, or toward thanksgiving and greater faithfulness.

Then the bridge widens the view. The God who created the heavens has taken notice of a single life. Psalm 8:3–4 marvels at that very contrast: “When I consider thy heavens, the work of thy fingers”, the psalmist asks, “What is man, that thou art mindful of him?” We cannot measure the wonder of His attention by the size of our assignment. A calling may appear ordinary to the world and still be worthy of wholehearted obedience.

But the bridge also speaks of freedom from the cost of transgressions. This is the foundation beneath every good work. Scripture teaches that salvation is not earned by faithful service: “For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works” (Ephesians 2:8–9, KJV). The very next verse says we are “created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them” (Ephesians 2:10, KJV). Grace comes first. Good works follow as its fruit, not its purchase price.

That order matters when the song says the task is “the thing you were born to do.” We may look back and discern how God has prepared and equipped us, but we do not need to claim perfect knowledge of His hidden purposes. We test our sense of calling against Scripture, pursue love and faithfulness, and entrust the results to Him. First Corinthians 3:6–7 offers a needed balance: “I have planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase.” We are responsible to serve; God is responsible for the growth.

Finally, the question “Who knew?” receives its answer: “Lord... is You.” That ending does not celebrate our ability to predict the future. It celebrates the Lord who knows us completely. Psalm 139:1 says, “O LORD, thou hast searched me, and known me.” And Philippians 1:6 directs our confidence toward “he which hath begun a good work in you” and His faithfulness to continue it. The most fitting response to a fruitful season is neither pride nor fear of losing it. It is worship, gratitude, and willingness to keep serving.

If things are going right today, thank God for that gift. If you are seeing fruit from years of quiet work, rejoice without taking the glory for yourself. And if the fruit is not yet visible, remember that obedience is not wasted simply because the harvest is still hidden. The Lord who knows the beginning also knows what He will do with a life offered to Him.

Prayer

Father, thank You for the grace that saves me and for the work You have prepared for me to do. Keep me close to Christ, so that any fruit in my life grows from Him. Guard me from pride when things go well and from discouragement when results are hidden. Let the joy of serving You be greater than the joy of being noticed. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
