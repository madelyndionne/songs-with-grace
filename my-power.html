Devotional

Before We Begin

The song, My Power, uses the machinery of a car to picture something Scripture teaches in much plainer language: we remember what is behind us, we think about what lies ahead, but the life God has actually given us to live is the life in front of us now. The song supplies the metaphor. Scripture supplies the truth.

The Past Can Teach Without Driving

Memory is useful. Scripture itself repeatedly tells God’s people to remember what He has done. Psalm 77:11–12 turns remembrance into worship, and Deuteronomy 8 warns against forgetting the Lord’s faithfulness.

But memory becomes a poor driver when yesterday is allowed to control today. Paul speaks of “forgetting those things which are behind” as he presses toward what lies ahead (Philippians 3:13–14). He is not erasing memory. He is refusing to let the past become the place where he lives.

The rearview mirror has a purpose. It was never meant to become the windshield.

The Future Can Motivate Without Owning Us

Planning is not condemned in Scripture. Proverbs commends wise preparation, and Jesus Himself teaches us to count the cost. But Matthew 6:34 places a boundary around anxiety about tomorrow: tomorrow has its own concerns; today has enough of its own.

That is an important distinction. Faith can look ahead without trying to live there.

The future may give direction, but it cannot give today’s strength. God meets us in the obedience, prayer, work, rest, and decisions that are actually before us.

God Is Present in the Present

The chorus of the song, My Power, points toward the Spirit as the power of present living. Scripture grounds that idea in the indwelling presence of God. Jesus promised that the Spirit would abide with His people (John 14:16–17). Romans 8:14 speaks of believers being led by the Spirit of God, and Galatians 5:25 calls us to walk in the Spirit.

That walking happens one step at a time.

We often want enough power for the whole road at once. God ordinarily gives grace for the next act of faithfulness.

Prayer Is Not a Fuel Gauge

The bridge playfully imagines regenerative braking putting “Prayer Power” back in. Prayer is not a mechanical recharge process, but the picture can still point us toward a biblical practice: slowing down enough to turn our attention back toward God.

Philippians 4:6–7 directs believers to bring their concerns to God in prayer, with thanksgiving, and promises the guarding peace of God. First Thessalonians 5:17 simply says, “Pray without ceasing.”

Prayer does not persuade God to become present. It helps us live consciously before the God who already is.

Grace Covers More Than the Mileage

The final image in the song is the extended warranty of grace. The metaphor is deliberately humorous, but grace itself is not a backup plan added after our strength fails. Ephesians 2:8–10 places grace at the beginning of salvation and then carries us into the life God prepared for us to walk in.

Second Corinthians 12:9 gives another angle: God’s grace is sufficient precisely where human strength reaches its limits.

We do not arrive home because the machine never wore down. We arrive because the One who called us is faithful.

Reflection

Am I spending more of today reliving yesterday or rehearsing tomorrow than actually living before God now?

What is one thing God has placed in front of me today that deserves my full attention?

Where do I need to slow down, pray, and let my thoughts come back under God’s direction?

Am I treating grace as something I need only when I fail, or as the foundation of the whole journey?

Prayer

Father, thank You for what You have taught me through the past and for the hope You have given me for the future. Keep me from living in either one. Teach me to walk with You today, led by Your Spirit, attentive to Your presence, and willing to slow down enough to pray. When my strength runs low, remind me that Your grace is sufficient. Keep me faithful for the road You have given me, one day at a time. Amen.
