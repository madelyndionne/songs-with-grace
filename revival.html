Devotional

Revival

The psalmist asks, “Wilt thou not revive us again: that thy people may rejoice in thee?” (Psalm 85:6). Revival, then, is not simply increased activity, emotional excitement, or a crowded church. The prayer is directed toward God, and the people being revived are His people. Revival is God working among His people in such a way that hearts awaken, truth becomes urgent, and people begin responding to Him.

The song, Revival, begins before anyone is certain what is happening: “I feel a rumbling / Not sure what it can be.” Sometimes the first evidence of spiritual movement is remarkably quiet. Someone begins listening differently. A familiar Scripture suddenly reaches deeper. A person who has remained seated decides to move. What appears small from the outside may represent something profound occurring within a heart.

When the Word Reaches the Heart

Acts 2 gives us a biblical picture of people responding when God's truth reaches them. After Peter preached Christ, Scripture says, “when they heard this, they were pricked in their heart” and asked, “Men and brethren, what shall we do?” (Acts 2:37). Peter answered, “Repent” (Acts 2:38), and those who received the word continued in “the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers” (Acts 2:42).

The preaching mattered. The listening mattered. The response mattered. But none of those things gives human beings ownership of what God was doing. The song, Revival, describes a preacher teaching, a congregation reaching, and people moving forward. Those visible actions can accompany awakening, but the biblical pattern directs attention beyond the atmosphere of the gathering toward repentance, truth, continuing in the Word, fellowship, prayer, and changed lives.

Test the Rumbling

That distinction matters whenever we begin to sense a spiritual “rumbling.” We should neither dismiss every stirring nor automatically declare every emotional moment to be revival. Jesus said of the Spirit, “he will guide you into all truth” (John 16:13). Whatever awakening God brings will agree with His Word and direct us toward Christ rather than merely toward an experience.

Emotion is not the enemy. Humility, conviction, joy, grief over sin, gratitude, and wonder may all accompany genuine spiritual response. But emotion cannot authenticate itself. Scripture remains the measure. An awakening that comes from God will not require us to set aside His truth in order to preserve the feeling.

The song moves from “Not sure what it can be” toward “Like a new beginning to me.” That progression is important. Spiritual awakening becomes increasingly recognizable through its fruit. Hearts soften. Pride gives way to humility. People hunger for Scripture. Sin becomes harder to excuse. Forgiveness becomes easier to offer. Service becomes less about recognition. Love begins reaching beyond the walls where the awakening started.

Revival Comes Close to Home

It is easy to watch a congregation and wonder whether revival is beginning among them. James turns the question toward us: “Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you” (James 4:8). Before asking whether something large is happening around us, we can ask whether we ourselves are becoming more responsive to God.

Am I drawing nearer to Him? Am I becoming more responsive to His Word? Is there something He has been asking me to surrender? Is my love becoming deeper, wider, and less concerned with myself? Am I willing to repent when Scripture exposes something in me, or am I more interested in watching what God appears to be doing in everyone else?

Perhaps that is part of the humbling in the song, Revival. If God is moving, we cannot claim ownership of it. We may teach, sing, pray, encourage, listen, or simply step into an aisle—but God remains the One who changes hearts. Paul expressed the same dependence when he wrote, “I have planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase” (1 Corinthians 3:6).

Ready to Respond

We do not need to manufacture the rumbling. We need to be willing to respond when God's Word calls us. Revival is not proved by how loudly a room reacts, how many people move at once, or how extraordinary a moment feels. Its deeper evidence appears in people who continue afterward—repenting, believing, learning, praying, loving, obeying, and following Christ.

Perhaps revival can begin very quietly indeed: one heart awakened, one person humbled, one obedient response—and then another.

Prayer

Lord, if You are stirring something among us, help me not merely to watch it happen around me. Awaken my heart. Show me what needs to change, what needs to be surrendered, and where Your Word is calling me to respond. Keep me grounded in Scripture so that I recognize truth and do not mistake emotion alone for Your leading. Humble me enough to know that the increase belongs to You, not to us.

Deepen my love for You and let that love reach outward to others. Give me a heart willing to repent, obey, pray, serve, and follow Christ beyond the moment of awakening. And when obedience requires me to take a visible step, give me the courage to stand and move. Amen.
