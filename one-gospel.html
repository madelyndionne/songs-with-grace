3-Day Devotional

Days 123

Day 1 - Not Ours to Design

Scripture: Galatians 1:11-12

Paul makes an extraordinary claim about the Gospel he preached: it wasn't something he invented, nor was it something another person simply taught him. Its source was Jesus Christ.

That distinction matters.

We are accustomed to shaping things to suit us. We adjust recipes, rearrange rooms, rewrite instructions, and change plans when we think we've found a better way. Much of the time, that's perfectly reasonable.

But the Gospel isn't ours to improve.

The opening verse of the song, One Gospel, puts it plainly:

This message did not come from man,
Not fashioned by a human mind.
Christ revealed His saving plan,
His truth was never ours to design.

That closely follows Paul's emphasis in Galatians 1:11-12: the Gospel he preached was not after man; he received it through the revelation of Jesus Christ.

That gives us both security and responsibility.

Security, because the Gospel doesn't depend upon our ability to invent something good enough to save us.

Responsibility, because if it came from God, we don't have authority to redesign it when some part becomes inconvenient, unpopular, or difficult.

Before we can recognize when we've drifted from the Gospel, we have to know whose Gospel it is.

It is possible to become so familiar with Christianity that we begin treating it as though it belongs to us. We can have opinions about how church should look, how worship should sound, what traditions should remain, and what should change. Those things aren't necessarily the Gospel.

The Gospel has an Author.

And perhaps that is the first question Galatians 1 asks us to settle:

Do I receive the Gospel as something God has given me - or am I quietly trying to make it into something I would rather have?

Prayer

Father, thank You that Your truth does not depend upon me to create it. Help me receive what You have given rather than reshape it according to my own desires. Give me humility to recognize Your truth, wisdom to understand it, and faithfulness to remain in it. Keep Christ at the center of what I believe and how I live. Amen.

Day 2 - Drift Doesn't Always Look Like Leaving

Scripture: Galatians 1:6-7

Paul's alarm in Galatians 1 is not directed at people who announced that they wanted nothing more to do with Christ. He is writing to people who were being drawn toward a different gospel - something that still sounded religious, but was no longer the Gospel they had received.

That is what makes drift dangerous. It does not always announce itself as departure.

The second verse of the song, One Gospel, describes the change in small, almost ordinary terms:

Some will change what they have heard,
Adding something of their own.
Till the message lacks His Word
And Christ is no longer on His throne.

Sometimes the change begins with addition rather than rejection. We keep Christ, but add another requirement for feeling accepted. We keep grace, but quietly add performance. We keep faith, but begin measuring ourselves by approval, activity, achievement, comfort, or the expectations of other people.

The added thing may even look good. Christian activity can be good. Serving can be good. Discipline can be good. The problem comes when something good begins doing a job that belongs to the Gospel - when it becomes the thing that tells us whether we are acceptable, secure, worthy, or faithful.

Then the center has shifted.

That kind of drift can be difficult to notice precisely because we may still be using Christian words and doing Christian things. Yet Paul's concern is not merely what surrounds the Gospel. His concern is whether the Gospel itself is being changed.

The chorus of the song, One Gospel, makes the loss personal: the One who called us into grace is the One we leave when we exchange His Gospel for another. The issue is not simply getting a doctrine wrong. It is moving away from the grace of the One who called us.

So perhaps the question for today is not only, "Have I left the Gospel?" It may be more revealing to ask:

What have I added to the Gospel that God never asked me to carry?

Is there something I have begun trusting to prove that I am doing well with God - my performance, someone else's approval, how busy I am, what I accomplish, or how religious my life appears?

Recognizing drift is not an invitation to panic. It is an invitation to return to the center: Christ, His grace, and the Gospel we received.

Prayer

Father, show me where I have added my own requirements to what You have given. Help me recognize anything that has begun competing with Christ for the center of my faith. Bring me back to Your grace, and teach me to rest in the Gospel rather than in my performance, approval, or activity. Keep my heart close to You and my trust fixed on Christ. Amen.

Day 3 - Measure It

Scripture: Galatians 1:8-10

Paul does something striking in Galatians 1. He does not tell the Galatians to accept a message because the messenger is impressive, familiar, sincere, or spiritual. He gives them a standard that stands above the messenger.

He makes the test deliberately extreme: even if Paul himself returned with a different gospel - even if an angel from heaven appeared with one - the message would still have to be rejected if it contradicted the Gospel they had received.

The bridge of the song, One Gospel, puts that test into four lines:

Even should an angel come,
With another gospel to proclaim,
Measure it by the Word of the Son—
The truth of Christ remains the same.

That word measure matters. We are not asked to decide what is true by asking who said it, how confidently it was said, how many people agree with it, how moving it sounded, or how well it fits what we already wanted to believe. The messenger may be gifted. The presentation may be beautiful. The argument may be persuasive. None of those things gets to become the measuring stick.

The Gospel is measured against the truth already given in Christ.

That requires more than suspicion. It requires familiarity. We cannot measure a message against a Gospel we barely know. Discernment grows as we remain close to Christ, read Scripture, remember grace, and learn the shape of the Gospel well enough to recognize when something has been added, removed, or moved away from the center.

Galatians 1:10 brings the test even closer to home. Paul asks whether he is trying to please people or God. Approval can become its own measuring stick. If I need a message to keep people pleased with me - or to keep me pleased with myself - I may be tempted to soften what is difficult, emphasize what is popular, or quietly reshape truth until it fits the room.

But truth does not become more true because people applaud it, and it does not become less true because they do not.

So the final purpose of discernment is not to make us suspicious of everyone. It is to help us remain faithful to the One who called us by grace. The final chorus of the song, One Gospel, no longer asks what has been changed. It gives the destination:

To My gospel, remain true,
The saving truth I gave to you.

Perhaps that is the question with which this study should end:

What am I using as my measure - Christ and His Word, or the voice that is easiest for me to believe?

When something sounds spiritual, persuasive, comforting, frightening, popular, or new, am I willing to measure it before I receive it?

Prayer

Father, teach me to measure what I hear by the truth You have given in Christ. Keep me from being carried by impressive voices, popular opinion, fear, or my own desire for approval. Give me a growing knowledge of Your Word and a heart that remains close to Jesus. When I encounter something that sounds true, help me test it faithfully. When I recognize Your truth, give me courage to remain in it. Keep me faithful to the saving Gospel You have given. Amen.

Extra Info

Galatians 1 Scripture Map

Galatians 1:6-7 — Paul is astonished that believers are turning from the One who called them in grace toward a different gospel. This is the concern carried by the chorus and Day 2.

Galatians 1:8-9 — Even an angelic messenger does not have authority to replace the Gospel already received. This is the direct foundation of the bridge.

Galatians 1:10 — Paul contrasts seeking human approval with serving Christ. Day 3 uses this as a second test of what is shaping our message and our response to truth.

Galatians 1:11-12 — Paul insists that the Gospel he preached was not fashioned by man but received through the revelation of Jesus Christ. This is the foundation of Verse 1 and Day 1.

Galatians 1:15-16 — Paul again speaks of God's grace, calling, and the revelation of His Son. The chapter keeps returning to the same center: God calls, Christ is revealed, and the Gospel is received rather than invented.
