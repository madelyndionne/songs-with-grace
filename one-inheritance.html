Devotional

Before We Begin

The song, One Inheritance, asks a question many believers eventually face: if salvation cannot be earned, what exactly has God given us in Christ? The song points toward adoption, belonging, and inheritance. Scripture—not the song—defines those truths, especially in Galatians 3–4 and Romans 8.

From Outsider to Child

We naturally understand achievement. We work, qualify, improve, and hope eventually to deserve what we seek. The gospel overturns that pattern. Ephesians 2:8–9 places salvation in the category of grace received through faith, not wages earned by works. Titus 3:4–7 makes the same distinction: salvation rests on God's mercy, and those justified by His grace become heirs according to the hope of eternal life.

That means Christian belonging begins with what God has done, not with a spiritual résumé we present to Him.

Galatians 4:4–7 goes further. In Christ, believers are not merely pardoned outsiders allowed to stand at the edge of the household. Paul speaks of adoption. The one who was formerly under bondage is received as a son, and because a son, an heir through God.

The change is not cosmetic. It is a change of relationship.

An Inheritance That Cannot Be Earned

An inheritance is different from a paycheck. A paycheck is earned because of work performed. An inheritance comes because of relationship and the will of the one who gives it.

Romans 8:14–17 uses that family language directly. Those led by the Spirit of God are described as children of God, and if children, then heirs—heirs of God and joint-heirs with Christ. The passage does not teach that believers have achieved equality with Christ; it teaches that their inheritance exists because they belong to Him.

This is why the question “Am I good enough?” cannot be answered by trying harder to become worthy of grace. Grace, by definition, is not earned. The believer's confidence rests in Christ and in the promises God attaches to union with Him.

Abraham and the Promise

The bridge of the song reaches back to Abraham, and Galatians 3 explains why. Paul connects justification by faith with Abraham and says that those who are of faith are blessed with faithful Abraham (Galatians 3:6–9). Later, he identifies the promised Seed ultimately with Christ and explains that those who belong to Christ are Abraham's seed and heirs according to promise (Galatians 3:16, 26–29).

That does not mean every historical promise made to Abraham becomes an identical individual promise to every Christian. Scripture itself determines how each promise is fulfilled. But Paul makes one point unmistakable: through Christ, believers who once stood outside are brought into the family of faith and made heirs according to God's promise.

The inheritance comes through Christ.

Belonging Changes the Question

Once we understand adoption, the question begins to change. Instead of asking, “Have I done enough to make God receive me?” the believer learns to ask, “How should I live because God has received me in Christ?”

Obedience still matters deeply. Jesus calls His people to follow Him, and the New Testament repeatedly calls believers to holiness, love, endurance, and good works. But obedience is not the purchase price of adoption. It is the life that grows from belonging to the Father.

We do not obey in order to buy a place in the family.

We learn to obey because, in Christ, God has brought us into His family.

Reflection

Do I still relate to God as though I were trying to earn admission rather than living from what Christ has already provided?

Where am I tempted to measure my standing with God primarily by my recent success or failure?

What changes when I remember that Scripture describes believers in Christ as God's children and heirs?

How can gratitude for grace shape my obedience today?

Prayer

Father, thank You that salvation is not something I can earn. Help me rest my confidence in Christ rather than in my own record. Teach me to understand what it means to be received as Your child and made an heir according to Your promise. Let that belonging produce gratitude, humility, obedience, and love in me. Keep my eyes on Christ, through whom every gift of grace comes. Amen.
