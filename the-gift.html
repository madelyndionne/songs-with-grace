Devotional

The Gift

There is something special about preparing a gift for someone.

Long before the package is opened, the giver has already been thinking about the person who will receive it. There may have been planning, searching, making, wrapping, expense, and time. The recipient may know nothing about any of it. But the gift has already occupied a place in the giver’s thoughts.

And then it is offered.

That is where the song, The Gift, begins with something very ordinary: an old lady spending part of each year preparing things for others. She organizes her workshop, makes her plans, pays the bills, fills a need, sends the packages—and waits.

She expects nothing in payment. The gifts were never transactions.

But that does not mean she does not care whether they are received.

When no message comes, her “little old lady heart” squeezes a bit. Not because anyone owes her something, but because what she prepared in love has gone out of her hands, and she does not know whether it has truly been received.

There is a profound difference between a gift being given and a gift being received.

A package can sit unopened. Something prepared especially for us can remain untouched. Its value does not disappear. The giver’s intention does not change. The price has still been paid.

But the recipient has not yet taken hold of what was offered.

The song uses that simple human experience to point toward something infinitely greater—not because our gifts can compare with God's, but because sometimes an ordinary thing helps us understand a spiritual truth.

God's gift of salvation is not wages for good behavior. It is not payment for service. It cannot be purchased with generosity, church attendance, religious knowledge, or a sufficiently respectable life. Scripture is emphatic that salvation is by grace through faith, not something we earn through works. (Ephesians 2:8–9; Romans 3:23–24; Romans 6:23)

And this Gift was not inexpensive.

Before we ever reached for it, God knew its cost. Christ gave Himself for us. The debt we could not pay was not ignored; it was borne by Christ through His sacrifice on the Cross. (Romans 5:8; 1 Peter 2:24; 1 Corinthians 15:3–4)

That changes the meaning of free.

Free does not mean without value.

Free does not mean without cost.

It means the cost is not being charged to the recipient.

That is grace.

Yet a gift may be freely offered and still remain unopened.

Scripture speaks not merely of knowing that Christ came, but of receiving Him and believing on His name. (John 1:12; John 3:16–17) We can know about the Gift. We can admire the wrapping. We can hear other people describe what they have received. We can even understand the theology of salvation remarkably well.

And still leave the Gift unopened.

Perhaps that is the quiet question beneath the song, The Gift.

What are we doing with what God has already offered?

For the person who has never received Christ, the invitation remains. Salvation is not something to earn first and receive later. We come with empty hands.

And for those who have already received Him, there is another reason to remember the Gift. We can begin living as though we must somehow repay God—working harder, doing more, trying to make ourselves worthy of something that was given precisely because we could never make ourselves worthy of it.

Grace does not leave us unchanged. It produces gratitude, obedience, love, and service. But those things are the response to the Gift, not its purchase price. (Titus 2:11–14; Ephesians 2:10)

The little package in the song waits for someone to open it.

The greater Gift has already been prepared.

The price has already been paid.

The offer has already been made.

Nothing owed. Nothing left to earn.

The question is not whether the Gift has been offered.

The question is whether it has been received.

Prayer

Father, thank You for the Gift of salvation that I could never earn and could never repay. Thank You for loving me enough to send Your Son and for the price Christ willingly paid for me on the Cross. Help me to receive Your grace with humble faith and to live in gratitude for what You have freely given. May my obedience, love, and service never become an attempt to earn Your favor, but always be my response to the grace I have already received. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
