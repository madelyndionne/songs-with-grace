Devotional

In the Hand of the Master

By Madelyn Dionne

“I am but the carbon in the pencil, The pencil in His hand...”

A carpenter's pencil is an unusual thing. Flat, rectangular, often worn and battered, it looks nothing like the familiar pencil we use for writing. Yet its shape serves a purpose. It stays where it is laid, marks rough surfaces, and fits the work for which it was made.

But this song goes a little further than the pencil itself.

It speaks of being willing to be something as small as the carbon inside it.

Something easily overlooked. Something that might seem insignificant. Yet without that small bit of carbon, the pencil cannot leave its mark.

And if God has chosen us for a particular task, however small it may appear, there is dignity in being chosen.

Scripture reminds us:

“For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.” — Ephesians 2:10 (KJV)

We are His workmanship, and He has prepared work for us to do.

Chosen for the Task

We sometimes measure the importance of work by how visible it is, how many people notice, or how impressive the results appear.

But a task does not have to be grand to be important to God.

A kind word. A small kindness. A quiet act of service. A responsibility faithfully carried out when nobody is watching.

If that is the work God has given us, it deserves our willingness and our care.

Jesus taught:

“He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much...” — Luke 16:10 (KJV)

There is no need to make ourselves bigger to make our service worthwhile.

The pencil doesn't have to become the carpenter. The whisk doesn't have to become the cook. The brush doesn't have to understand the finished design.

Each has its place in the work.

And we can be content with ours.

The Joy of Being Useful

“I don't need to be bigger... To accomplish His work.”

There is freedom in those words.

We don't have to compete for a more impressive assignment. We don't have to surrender the peculiarities that make us who we are. We can simply be willing to do what God has placed before us.

And there is nothing wrong with enjoying it.

In fact, there can be a particular satisfaction in knowing that even an ordinary task is being done for Him.

“And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men.” — Colossians 3:23 (KJV)

That changes the way we approach our work.

The task may be small, repetitive, unnoticed, or imperfectly accomplished. But the One for whom we do it is worthy of our best effort.

We can take pleasure in serving Him without needing applause, recognition, or a larger assignment.

The Hand That Holds the Pencil

The chorus of Utensils expresses a simple understanding:

“I'm just the carbon, not the hand, I can't see the whole of His plan.”

The carbon leaves the mark, but the hand determines where that mark belongs.

We may never see how our small contribution fits into God's larger purposes. We may be asked to do something that seems unremarkable, only to discover later that it mattered far more than we imagined.

And sometimes we may never discover its significance at all.

Yet God invites us to call upon Him:

“Call unto me, and I will answer thee, and shew thee great and mighty things, which thou knowest not.” — Jeremiah 33:3 (KJV)

There is so much we do not know. But the One who holds us knows.

Our part is to be willing.

And through it all, our desire is that the work would draw attention to Him rather than ourselves.

“He must increase, but I must decrease.” — John 3:30 (KJV)

Less of me. More of You.

A Small Personal Wonder

When Utensils was finally finished, its final musical version needed no further revisions. On the very first listen, it sounded just as I had hoped.

Then I noticed its length.

Three minutes and thirty-three seconds. 3:33.

My thoughts went immediately to Jeremiah 33:3.

I cannot explain why the recording happened to finish at that particular moment. But I can tell you what it brought to mind: the invitation to call upon God and the promise that He knows things beyond my understanding.

It was a fitting moment of reflection for a song about being a small instrument in His hand.

And it made me smile.

A Prayer

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for choosing me to serve You, even in the smallest ways.

Help me to recognize the value of the tasks You place before me, whether anyone else notices them or not.

Keep me willing, faithful, and content with the work You have given me. Let me find satisfaction and joy in doing it for You.

I do not need to be the hand that directs the work. I am grateful simply to be useful in Yours.

In Jesus' name, Amen.

Scripture for Further Reflection: Ephesians 2:10; Luke 16:10; Colossians 3:23–24; 1 Corinthians 12:4–11; Jeremiah 33:3; John 3:30 (KJV).
