As the Worm Turns

Devotional · Creation, Joy & Humor

A Little Context Before You Listen

We raise worms to help enrich the soil in our garden. Like most living things, they occasionally provide considerably more entertainment than their job description requires.

As the Worm Turns is not the usual fare around here. It grew out of watching the very real—and sometimes surprisingly dramatic—goings-on in our worm bin. Somewhere along the way, observation became a soap opera, and the soap opera became a song.

It simply had to come out.

So, with a smile firmly in place, meet the cast of As the Worm Turns.

Devotional

Paying Attention

Scripture begins by telling us that God made “the living creature after his kind”, including the creatures that move close to the ground (Genesis 1:24–25). Job later says, “ask now the beasts, and they shall teach thee”, and reminds us that “in whose hand is the soul of every living thing” (Job 12:7–10). We do not have to make something grand before it can remind us to notice the world He made.

The song, As the Worm Turns, starts in one of the least glamorous places imaginable: a worm bin. It notices escapes, growth, tiny new worms, strange biology, and the ordinary cycle of life. Then it does something wonderfully human with what it notices—it laughs.

That laughter does not make the subject insignificant. Psalm 104:24 marvels, “O LORD, how manifold are thy works! in wisdom hast thou made them all”. A worm may be small and hidden, but it is not outside His creation. Its design, its place in the soil, and even the details that make us grin are part of a world far more intricate than we usually stop to observe.

Wonder Does Not Have to Be Solemn

Sometimes reverence looks like silence. Sometimes it looks like amazement. And sometimes our attention to creation ends in delighted laughter. Proverbs 17:22 says, “A merry heart doeth good like a medicine”. Joy is not the enemy of seriousness; it can be one way we receive ordinary life with gratitude.

This song is not a coded allegory in which every feature of worm biology represents a doctrine. Scripture remains the authority. The worms simply give us an excuse to pay attention—to see that even the small, easily overlooked corners of creation can awaken curiosity, humility, gratitude, and joy.

That may be the quiet invitation underneath all the silliness: look closer. There is more happening around us than we notice. A garden, a bird, a seed, a worm bin—ordinary things can remind us that God's world is active, layered, surprising, and worth receiving with open eyes.

For Reflection

What ordinary part of creation have I stopped noticing? When was the last time something small made me curious, grateful, or simply glad? Can I allow wholesome humor and wonder to coexist in the way I receive God's world?

Prayer

Lord, thank You for the abundance and variety of Your creation. Give me eyes to notice what I normally overlook and a heart that responds with gratitude. Keep my joy wholesome, my curiosity alive, and my attention turned toward You, the Maker of all things. Amen.
