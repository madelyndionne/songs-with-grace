Devotional

Willow Water

Some plants seem remarkably willing to begin again. A willow branch can be cut from the tree that sustained it, separated from the roots that once supplied it, and placed somewhere entirely new. To the eye, it may look like little more than a severed branch. But the story is not necessarily over. Something is already present within it that can contribute to the formation of new roots.

That ordinary observation gives the song, Willow Water, its central image: “What is living deep within / The willow helps new roots begin.” There is something profoundly hopeful about those words because there are seasons in human life when we can feel much like the cutting. Something familiar has been severed. A relationship ends. Someone we love dies. Health changes. A place that once felt like home no longer exists. A disappointment alters the future we expected. Sometimes there is no dramatic event at all—we simply realize that the ground beneath us has changed. And we wonder: How do I grow from here?

Someone Is Living Within

For the Christian, the answer begins with something far greater than our own ability to recover. Jesus promised His disciples another Comforter, “that he may abide with you for ever”, and said, “he dwelleth with you, and shall be in you” (John 14:16–17). Paul likewise says, “if the Spirit of him that raised up Jesus from the dead dwell in you”, the God who raised Christ will give life through His indwelling Spirit (Romans 8:11).

That means when circumstances leave us feeling uprooted, we have not been emptied of the presence of God. Someone is living within. The Holy Spirit does not merely visit when life is flourishing and withdraw when we are broken. Jesus said He would abide with His people forever.

We should be precise here. The Holy Spirit is not a hidden human resource that we activate when we need resilience. He is not an impersonal force stored within us. He is God dwelling within His people. The willow can illustrate something about hidden life and new roots; it cannot define the Spirit.

Roots Begin Where Nobody Sees

We often look for evidence of God's work above the surface. We want new leaves. We want visible growth. We want circumstances repaired and prayers answered in ways we can recognize. Yet much of what Scripture describes as spiritual strengthening occurs within.

Paul prays that believers would be “strengthened with might by his Spirit in the inner man”, that Christ would dwell in their hearts by faith, and that they would be “rooted and grounded in love” (Ephesians 3:16–17). Colossians says, “As ye have therefore received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk ye in him: Rooted and built up in him” (Colossians 2:6–7).

Roots are not glamorous. They are largely hidden. But they establish what will later have to stand in wind and weather. We should not assume that every period without visible change means God is secretly producing the particular outcome we want. Scripture does not promise that. But it does tell us where our rootedness belongs: in Christ, strengthened by His Spirit.

What We Receive Can Become Care for Another

The willow gives the song another part of its picture. What it carries can be shared. The song, Willow Water, moves from the willow forming its own roots to something from the willow being used in the care of another cutting: “As what the willow freely shares / With other cuttings needing care, / The Spirit works through us for others— / Extending healing through our care.”

Scripture gives us a clear pattern for receiving and then extending care. Paul calls God “the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort”, who “comforteth us in all our tribulation” so that “we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble” with the comfort we ourselves have received from God (2 Corinthians 1:3–4).

Likewise, Galatians tells us, “Bear ye one another's burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ” (Galatians 6:2). We receive—and then we give. We are strengthened—and then we strengthen. Someone helps carry us—and eventually we may find ourselves able to help carry someone else.

The pain itself is not what qualifies us. God's work within the pain can make us more able to recognize and care for another person's need. A person who has grieved may recognize grief in another person's eyes before anyone else notices. Someone who has known loneliness may understand the importance of simply staying. Someone who has struggled with doubt may know how to sit beside a questioning person without becoming frightened by the questions. Someone who has received mercy may become unusually generous in giving it.

The Illustration Has Limits

We should not carry the willow metaphor farther than Scripture allows. The Holy Spirit is not an impersonal substance or spiritual growth compound. He is God. Nor does Scripture promise that every wound will disappear in this life, that every broken circumstance will be restored in the form we desire, or that our care itself possesses healing power.

The willow is only an illustration. Scripture is the authority. And Scripture makes something wonderfully clear: God gives His Spirit to His people. He strengthens us inwardly. He roots us in Christ and in love. He comforts us in trouble. And He can work through the comfort, mercy, patience, and love He has formed in us as we care for someone else.

That changes how we might look at our broken places. Perhaps the question is not only, “Will I ever grow again?” Perhaps, in time, another question emerges: “How might God use what He has grown in me to help someone else?”

The final chorus of the song says, “Living deep within— / The Spirit helps new roots begin. / Healing what's been broken, / Helping all of us root again.” We can receive that image while keeping Scripture's order clear. We do not manufacture the life. We do not create the Spirit's power. We do not become someone else's Savior. We remain dependent upon God and available to serve.

And sometimes the person who once needed help finding solid ground becomes one of the people God uses to help another stand.

Prayer

Father, thank You for giving Your Spirit to dwell within Your people. When I feel uprooted, broken, or uncertain of where I belong, remind me that Your presence has not left me. Strengthen me in the inner person and root me more deeply in Christ and in Your love.

Bring healing according to Your wisdom, and keep me from claiming for myself what belongs to You alone. As You comfort, strengthen, and restore me, make me willing to share that comfort with others. Help me carry burdens gently, love faithfully, and remember that every good thing comes from You. May what You grow within me become useful in Your hands as I care for someone else who is trying to find solid ground. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Digging Deeper — Botanical Note

Willow is well known for its ability to root readily. Willow tissue contains biologically active compounds, including indole-3-acetic acid (IAA), a naturally occurring auxin involved in plant growth and root initiation, and salicylic acid-related compounds. Homemade willow extracts are used by gardeners as a rooting aid, although their effectiveness varies with plant species, concentration, preparation, and season.
