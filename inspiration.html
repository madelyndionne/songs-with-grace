Devotional

Inspiration

Sometimes we enter silence because we want something.

An answer. A line of music. A direction. A solution. A word that will finally make sense of what has been circling in the mind.

The song, Inspiration, begins there - with a crafter trying to make room for thought.

Quiet now... let the silence speak
There are treasures silence keeps
If I loosen my own control
Something deeper fills my soul

At first, the desired result still matters. The writer wants words. The mind is searching for meaning. But the song gradually discovers that the product may not be the most important part of the experience.

The deeper movement is the process of becoming receptive.

We remove what is extraneous. We quiet competing voices. We stop insisting that the moment must produce a particular result. We examine what rises in the mind and discard what does not belong. We become attentive.

That can be one entrance into Christian meditation.

Not an Empty Mind, but an Attentive One

Biblical meditation is not simply the attempt to make the mind blank. Scripture repeatedly directs meditation toward God, His Word, His works, and His ways. (Psalm 1:1-3; Psalm 77:11-12; Psalm 119:15-16; Psalm 143:5)

The goal is not mental emptiness for its own sake. It is attention.

There are many ways a believer may arrive at that attentive place. Scripture, prayer, worship, music, solitude, walking, grief, or ordinary work may each become occasions in which the noise recedes and our attention turns more deliberately toward Christ.

The song illustrates one such path. It does not prescribe a formula.

The common movement may be simpler: less distraction, less insistence on controlling the experience, more deliberate attention to God, and greater willingness to listen.

Releasing the Product

One of the most revealing lines in the song is: "The crafter seems to have less say."

The crafter has not disappeared. There is still thought, judgment, skill, choice, and discernment. But the need to dominate the process begins to loosen.

That resembles an important movement in prayer and meditation. We may begin by asking God for an answer, an outcome, or a particular experience. Yet intimacy deepens when the relationship itself becomes more important than the thing we hoped to obtain.

Jesus used the language of abiding. The branch does not manufacture fruit by straining harder. It remains in the vine, and fruit grows from that living relationship. (John 15:4-5)

That does not mean effort is unnecessary. It means relationship comes before production.

The question changes from What will I get from this time? to Whom am I attending to?

Listening and Discerning

The song also includes an essential safeguard:

Examining and discarding
Waiting for something to grow
Then listening and disarming.

Quietness does not make every thought trustworthy. An inward impression is not automatically the voice of God simply because it arose during prayer or meditation.

Scripture tells believers to test what they encounter spiritually. (1 Thessalonians 5:21; 1 John 4:1) God's written Word remains the governing authority. The Spirit of truth does not contradict the truth He has already given. (John 14:26; John 16:13-14)

So Christian listening includes discernment. We listen humbly, compare what we perceive with Scripture, and remain willing to release what does not withstand that test.

From Something to Some One

Then the final chorus changes only a few words, but those words reveal where the entire process has been leading:

Quiet now... let the Spirit speak
There are treasures His Spirit keeps
If I loosen my own control
Some One deeper fills my soul

At first, the singer was waiting for something deeper.

By the end, the discovery is Some One.

That is the difference between using silence merely as a technique and entering quietness as a posture of relationship.

The goal is not to create a mystical experience. We cannot manufacture intimacy with Christ by arranging the perfect atmosphere. Nor do we summon the Holy Spirit by becoming sufficiently quiet. For the believer, the Spirit already dwells within. (John 14:16-17; Romans 8:9)

But we can become more attentive to the presence and truth we so easily overlook when the mind is crowded with urgency, noise, and our own determination to control what happens next.

Perhaps this is why stillness can feel like an arrival even though we have gone nowhere.

We have not brought Christ closer.

We have become quieter before Him.

And sometimes, after loosening our grip on the product we thought we needed, we discover that the deeper gift was not the answer, the insight, or even the song.

It was intimacy with the One we were learning to hear.

Prayer

Father, quiet in me what keeps me from attending to You. Help me release the need to control every moment or demand a particular result from my time with You. Teach me to meditate on Your Word, to remember Your works, and to listen with humility. Give me discernment to test every thought and impression by the truth You have already given in Scripture. Keep me from seeking an experience more than I seek Christ. As the unnecessary noise falls away, deepen my awareness of Your presence and draw me into a closer, steadier relationship with You. In Jesus' name, Amen.
