Devotional

Belonging by Birth, Adoption, and Grace

What does it mean to be someone's child? We may enter an earthly family through birth or be welcomed into one through adoption. Both are real family relationships, carrying the gifts and responsibilities of belonging. God's command to honor father and mother (Exodus 20:12) invites us to take those relationships seriously. Yet Child Of God asks us to ponder another kind of belonging—one that reaches beyond our earthly lives.

Genesis 1:27 tells us that God created mankind in His image. That truth gives every person dignity, whatever their family circumstances. But being made in God's image and becoming His adopted child are distinct truths. Scripture describes adoption into God's family as a gift received through Jesus Christ (Ephesians 1:5; Galatians 4:4–7). Our heavenly family relationship is established by His grace, not by our ancestry or our achievements.

Earthly adoption offers a particularly tender picture of this truth. A child welcomed into a family is genuinely a son or daughter, not a guest waiting to earn a place. The commitment of adoption gives a new relationship its name and standing. Likewise, Romans 8:15–17 describes believers receiving the Spirit of adoption and becoming children and heirs of God. The comparison helps us glimpse the wonder, while the heavenly gift reaches further than any earthly analogy.

Look at the certificate on this song's cover. It is addressed simply to You. A small hand hovers above it, as though its owner is still trying to believe the words. The declaration is certain; the child is still taking in what it means. And the crimson seal draws our attention to the cost of our redemption: Ephesians 1:7 speaks of forgiveness and redemption through the blood of Jesus Christ. His blood—not our bloodline—makes this heavenly adoption possible.

Earthly parentage belongs to a life with beginnings and endings. God's promise to His children extends beyond this life. Romans 8:17 calls them heirs with Christ, and 1 Peter 1:3–4 describes an inheritance that does not fade away. The Christmas star in the distance reminds us of the Son who came into the world so that we might receive adoption (Galatians 4:4–5). What begins as a question about father and mother becomes a much larger question: have we considered the awesome wonder of being called a child of God?

Prayer

Heavenly Father, thank You for creating us in Your image and for the love and responsibility found in families formed through birth and adoption. Thank You for sending Jesus Christ, through whose blood we have redemption and through whom we may be welcomed into Your family. Help us honor our earthly parents, understand the gift of Your grace, and live as Your children with gratitude and faith. Keep alive in us the wonder of belonging to You. In Jesus' name, Amen.
