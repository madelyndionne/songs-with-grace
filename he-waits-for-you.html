Devotional

Before We Begin

The voice in the song, He Waits For You, might be the still, small voice of a person's own heart—speaking truths that have perhaps been known, resisted, questioned, or forgotten. The “you” is therefore deeply personal. It is the heart speaking inwardly: You knew. You chose. You wandered. And still, He waits.

When We Turn Away

We sometimes recognize the choice before us and still make the wrong one. James describes a person being “drawn away of his own lust, and enticed” (James 1:14), while 1 Corinthians assures us that with temptation God will “also make a way to escape” (1 Corinthians 10:13). When we fail, however, God is not surprised by what we have done. He knows us completely (Psalm 139:1–4), and failure does not mean that He has stopped calling us.

Scripture describes the Lord as “longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance” (2 Peter 3:9). Romans says that “the goodness of God leadeth thee to repentance” (Romans 2:4). God's patience is not approval of our wandering. It is an opportunity to turn around.

What Will Satisfy?

Isaiah asks a question that still reaches us today: “Wherefore do ye spend money for that which is not bread? and your labour for that which satisfieth not?” (Isaiah 55:2). We can spend years asking possessions, accomplishments, relationships, pleasure, or approval to satisfy needs they were never designed to fill.

Jesus offers something different. He told the woman at the well that the water He gives becomes “a well of water springing up into everlasting life” (John 4:14). And we do not have to earn our way to Him. Scripture tells us that “while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us” (Romans 5:8). The work of salvation begins not with what we can do for Christ, but with what Christ has already done for us. Our response can begin very simply: “For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved” (Romans 10:13).

He Does Not Force the Answer

God calls us to Himself, but Scripture also makes clear that we can resist that call. Jesus grieved over Jerusalem, saying He would have gathered its people to Himself, “and ye would not” (Matthew 23:37). In John 5:40 He said, “ye will not come to me, that ye might have life.” There is both love and choice in those words.

God's patience should not be mistaken for indifference. Hebrews urges us, “To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts” (Hebrews 3:15). Yet Scripture also shows how readily the returning one is received. In Jesus' account of the prodigal son, while the son was “yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran” (Luke 15:20). God calls. We choose whether to answer. And when we turn toward Him, we find mercy waiting.

A Prayer

Lord Jesus, I know there have been times when I have turned away from You. Thank You for Your patience and for loving me even when I have wandered. Help me hear Your voice and answer You today. Forgive me, guide me, and teach me to place my trust in You. Amen.

Share the Invitation

Is there someone in your life who may need to know that it is not too late to turn toward God? You do not need to have all the answers or find the perfect words. You might simply share the song, He Waits For You, along with one thought from Scripture that spoke to you, and then pray for that person. Sometimes sharing the invitation is as simple as letting someone know that the door is still open.

Extra Info — Scripture Map

Temptation and choice: James 1:14–15; 1 Corinthians 10:13

God knows us: Psalm 139:1–4

God's patience and repentance: 2 Peter 3:9; Romans 2:4

What truly satisfies: Isaiah 55:2; John 4:14

Christ died for sinners: Romans 5:8

Calling on the Lord: Romans 10:13

Responding rather than resisting: Matthew 23:37; John 5:40; Hebrews 3:15

Mercy when we return: Luke 15:11–24

These passages are the biblical foundation of the devotional. The song, He Waits For You, serves as an invitation to consider them personally; Scripture remains the authority for what we believe.
