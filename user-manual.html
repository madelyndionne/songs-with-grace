Devotional

User Manual

Some manuals are easy to follow. Others seem to contain everything except the answer to the problem you actually have. You can turn pages, study diagrams, reread instructions—and still wonder, What am I supposed to do now? Life can feel remarkably similar.

But the song, User Manual, turns that familiar frustration around and asks a much more personal question: “If your life became a manual, / What would someone learn from you?” Whether we realize it or not, people read our lives. They see how we respond when things go wrong. They notice how we treat someone who can do nothing for us. They watch what happens to our faith when prayers seem unanswered, when plans change, when we are hurt, disappointed, frightened, or simply tired.

Pages People Can Read

What are those pages teaching? Perhaps some are dog-eared because they contain lessons learned through difficulty. Some may have corrections written across them because experience taught us that what we once believed was wrong. There may be pages we wish we could tear out altogether.

But there are also pages of grace—places where God changed us, where forgiveness replaced resentment, where patience grew because we had no choice but to wait, or where we learned to trust God without knowing what He was doing. Perhaps there are pages whose importance we do not recognize at all, yet someone else has quietly highlighted them because something in the way we lived helped them find their own way forward.

Jesus tells His followers, “Ye are the light of the world” and says, “Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven” (Matthew 5:14, 16). A faithful life can indeed be seen. But Jesus directs the glory beyond the person being observed and toward the Father.

We Are Not the Holy Manual

That gives us reason to consider our lives carefully, but it does not make our lives the authority. We are not the Holy Manual. God has already given us His Word. The psalmist says, “Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path” (Psalm 119:105). Proverbs tells us, “Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding” and “In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths” (Proverbs 3:5–6).

Scripture does more than offer occasional advice. Paul writes, “All scripture is given by inspiration of God” and is “profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness”, so that the servant of God may be “throughly furnished unto all good works” (2 Timothy 3:16–17).

When we do not know what belongs on our next page, we do not have to invent truth. We return to what God has already given. Scripture speaks. Our lives are shaped by it.

From the Page Into a Life

James warns us, “be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only” (James 1:22). He compares the person who hears without doing to someone who looks at his face in a glass and then goes away, forgetting what he saw (James 1:23–24). God's Word is not given merely to be admired, highlighted, or discussed. It is meant to be obeyed.

And as we follow His instruction, something beautiful happens: the truth written in Scripture begins to become visible in an ordinary human life. Not perfectly. Not without crossed-out lines, stained pages, repentance, and lessons we sometimes have to learn more than once. But visibly.

Jesus says, “By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another” (John 13:35). Paul describes the Spirit's fruit as “love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance” (Galatians 5:22–23). Love becomes something another person can witness. Mercy becomes something another person can receive. Faithfulness becomes something another person can remember. Grace becomes something another person can recognize because they encountered its effects through us.

What Would They Highlight?

The song, User Manual, asks which pages someone would dog-ear and which words they would highlight. That is worth considering, but the deeper question is not whether our lives are interesting enough to be read. It is whether what others see in us increasingly agrees with the Word we claim to follow.

Our experiences may teach something. Our example may encourage someone. Our mistakes may warn them. Our faithfulness may strengthen them. But our lives remain illustrations, not revelation. Scripture is the authority; the life of a believer should increasingly become evidence of having listened to it.

So perhaps the question in the song is worth carrying with us: “If your life became a manual, / What would someone learn from you?” And perhaps an even deeper question follows: If someone read your life today, would its pages point them toward the One whose Word you are trying to follow?

Prayer

Father, thank You for giving me Your Word. When I do not know what belongs on the next page of my life, lead me back to what You have already spoken. Teach me not merely to hear Scripture, but to obey it. Correct what is wrong in me, strengthen what reflects Your truth, and keep shaping my life by Your instruction.

Let the people who read my life see more than my personality, accomplishments, failures, or opinions. Let them see the fruit of Your work in me. May my words and actions never compete with the authority of Scripture, but instead point beyond me to Christ and to the truth You have given. In Jesus' name, Amen.
