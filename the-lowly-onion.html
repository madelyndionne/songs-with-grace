Devotional

Growing Where Mercy Begins

It is of the LORD's mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness. — Lamentations 3:22–23 (KJV)

An onion is an ordinary thing. It grows beneath the soil, hidden from view, forming layer upon layer around a living center. Each layer becomes part of the whole. Some are tender, some sharp, but all belong to the same onion.

It needn’t be beautiful in the conventional sense to be valuable. It needn’t become something else to fulfill its purpose.

God created us in His image (Genesis 1:27). Each of us enters this world with abilities, characteristics, and possibilities that unfold over a lifetime. Our experiences shape us, and sometimes those experiences are painful. We develop protective layers. We learn ways of responding to the world that may serve us for a season, yet become burdensome as we grow.

But our identity is more than the sum of our experiences.

In The Lowly Onion, one line carries the heart of the message: “And called me to grow where Your mercies begin.”

Where do God’s mercies begin? They meet us in our need. We come to Him with our strengths and weaknesses, our gifts and limitations, our accomplishments and failures. His mercy gives us a place to begin again. And His mercies are new every morning.

This is the beautiful relationship between grace and growth. We do not earn God’s love through our accomplishments. We receive His love and respond by growing in faith and obedience.

Ephesians 2:8–10 reminds us that we are saved by grace through faith, and that we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works. We belong to Him, and our works become an expression of that belonging.

Romans 12:2 calls us to be transformed by the renewing of our minds. As God’s Word takes root within us, our understanding deepens. We learn to recognize where our thoughts, attitudes, and responses need to change.

James 1:21 speaks of receiving “the engrafted word, which is able to save your souls.” What a beautiful picture of something received that becomes part of a living, growing relationship with God.

The fruit of the Spirit, described in Galatians 5:22–23, provides a way to examine that growth: love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, and temperance.

We may recognize some of these qualities developing strongly in our lives while seeing others that need greater attention. Such awareness can become an invitation to prayer, humility, and continued growth.

And then there is the heat. Anyone who has slowly caramelized an onion knows what happens. The sharpness softens. The sweetness becomes more pronounced. The onion remains an onion, but its character has changed.

Our lives also pass through seasons of difficulty, waiting, and refinement. Through those seasons, God can teach us patience, deepen our compassion, strengthen our faith, and help us respond with greater love.

The song expresses this transformation: “Your heat draws sweetness from deep within, / Where sharpness and pain had always been. / Slowly transformed by Your Holy flame, / The onion remains, yet not quite the same.”

There is another lesson in the humble onion. Together with a carrot and celery, it becomes part of a mirepoix, a foundation for countless nourishing dishes. Each ingredient contributes its own character. None needs to become the other. Their differences make the whole richer.

So it is with the gifts God entrusts to us. First Peter 4:10 calls us to minister to one another as good stewards of His grace. Colossians 3:23 instructs us to do our work heartily, as unto the Lord.

We are called to offer what we have been given faithfully, whether our contributions are noticed by many or known only to a few.

There is freedom in accepting the person God created while welcoming the transforming work He continues to accomplish. We needn’t become someone else to be useful to Him. We need to be willing to grow where His mercies begin.

And sometimes, after many years of living, learning, and growing, we discover that the life God has been preparing us to live is the very life into which He has brought us. A life of purpose. A life of service. A life still growing in grace. And through Christ, a life with an eternal promise.

Prayer

Heavenly Father, thank You for creating me in Your image and for knowing my heart more fully than I know it myself. Thank You for Your mercies, renewed each morning, and for the grace through which You call me to grow. Help me recognize the gifts You have entrusted to me and use them faithfully. Teach me patience where I am impatient, gentleness where I am sharp, and humility where I rely too heavily upon my own strength. Let Your Word take root in my heart. Continue renewing my mind and cultivating the fruit of Your Spirit in my life. Through every season, help me trust Your transforming work. May I become more like Christ while faithfully offering the life You have given me. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

For Reflection

Where in your life do you most clearly recognize God’s mercy? What qualities of the fruit of the Spirit are growing, and which need further cultivation? Are you measuring your usefulness by the expectations of others, or by your faithfulness to what God has entrusted to you?
